# Right-size it: guide

Pick a routine task you actually have, choose a lighter model for it on purpose, and switch back if it turns out to need more.

> **Detection is not available yet.** Dram cannot recognize this pattern in your sessions today, so finishing this guide will not show a recognition. The guide is still worth doing: it shows you which of your routine work a lighter model handles.

## 1. Find a real routine task

Look at your own project for routine work you need to do anyway: a rename, docstrings, formatting, a version bump or a simple fix.

If you have no routine task right now, **stop here**. There is no exercise and nothing to recognize; come back when you next have one.

If you already use a lighter model as your usual model, this pattern is about choosing one **below** your usual tier; you may already be where it points.

## 2. Find out what your harness supports

Ask your agent:

> Which models does this harness actually support here, how do I switch between them in this session, and which one is lighter than the one I am using now?

Use the command, launch flag or setting it reports. Do not guess a model name or flag.

## 3. Take the step: switch before the work, then do the work

Switch to the lighter model **before** the routine work starts, then ask for the change as you normally would. If the task turns out to be harder than it looked, switch back on purpose.

**Done:** you chose the lighter model yourself and the agent then made the edits for the routine task on it. Your usual checks tell you whether the result is good enough.

**Not done yet:** if you switched but did no work on the lighter model, or you only read about models, or the harness switched models on its own after a limit, the pattern has not happened yet. That is fine. Nothing is recorded against you; try it on your next routine task.

## Later

Once several patterns happen on their own, you can make a lighter model the default for routine work, or pin your own command or subagent to one, with an explicit way to escalate hard tasks. That comes later, not as a shortcut.
